# 🧠 The Problem — "Algo Brain"

## What Is Algo Brain?

- "I call it algo brain, but in reality it's just that I think being online messes with your head. I think it's just a price that we have all come to accept."
- "We all know the internet is bad for you. But then why are we here? Just to suffer?"
- Not a clinical term — his shorthand for the cumulative cognitive damage from algorithmic content consumption

## The Brain Is Trainable (and Degradable)

- References a book he read about the brain: "There are certain elements to your brain that are just hardcoded. But there are also elements that can be trained, like your attention span."
- "And because of that, that also means it can degrade."
- Tech companies' metric of success is how much attention they can extract from you — "it's a race to the bottom"
- "That's why clicking a YouTube video is like getting shot at in a shotgun. Point blank rage. Bro, chill."
- "The more time you spend online, the more algo brain you got, the more sucking has happened to you."

## The Creator's Dilemma

- "5,000 engineers optimizing retention. And that job has been passed down to me."
- "The whole success of this YouTube video is how much I can keep you engaged."
- "What happened to just simple old clickbait? I got to engage people now."
- Acknowledges the irony of fighting the algorithm while being subject to it as a creator

## Gen Z Cognitive Decline

- "Did you know that Gen Z has the lowest cognitive performance ever? The first time it went down — we would always become smarter."
- "Who can blame them? Look at the environment we're in."
- Dismisses the counterargument that kids are "better at playing Minecraft"

## Roblox & Children

- "Games when I was a kid was fun. Now look at Roblox. The biggest game for children has microtransactions in it. That's weird."

## AI-Generated Shorts — "Industrialized Nothingness"

- "Now there is short form content. And just so we're clear, I am a fun person. I like a meme."
- "But what I am not okay with is AI-generated shorts which is being created so we got consumed with no intent. Created with no intent."
- "Congratulations. We've industrialized nothingness and it's just getting worse."

# 💊 The Cure — "The Cheat Code"

## The Philosophy: Intent Is Everything

- "The key is intent. That's what matters. Intent matters."
- "If you go around your life not making your own choices, then who the heck are you?"
- "You are your choices and your actions. That's what shapes who you are. We understood this from philosophy thousands of years ago."
- "But if you don't even make a choice and just let the algorithm dictate your life, you got like the MIB alien in your brain steering."
- Not anti-technology, not anti-fun: "If you want to play Roblox all day, knock yourself out. But the key is intent."

## Personal Example — Phone Without Intent

- "I used to catch myself all the time being on my phone without intending to and I get so pissed off"
- "It's like you opening the fridge. Same thing. I find myself just wasting time and I'll be like, I did not want to do this."
- "If you want to do this, that's good for you, but I don't want to do this."

# 🔧 Step-by-Step: The Tech Fence

> "I'm not saying you have to do all of it, but you should do some of it."
> 

## Step 1 — Second Profile on Phone (Friction)

- Started with his de-Googling video — put all untrusted apps in a **second profile** on his phone
- "Social media, anything attention hungry, it went on the second profile"
- The 5-6 second delay to switch profiles creates **friction** that breaks the autopilot
- "Those 5 to 6 seconds that it takes for me to switch over to the other profile stops me every time from going, 'Hey, is this what I want to be doing?'"
- "People get dumb phones to stop themselves from wasting time. But really, that's all you need. You just need a little bit of friction."
- "Now my phone is what I want it to be — a device that serves me, NOT THE OTHER WAY AROUND. I'M NOT THE PRODUCT."
- Main profile has: phone, text messages, email — essentials
- Second profile has: social media, attention-hungry apps — "the dark side" / "the rainbow sucking attention side"
- "When you do head over to the other side... it's so obvious what's going on. Everything is so desperate for your attention."

### De-Google Admission

- "Full transparency, I still use Google Maps. It's too good. It tracks traffic too good."
- Tried using a wrapper for Google Maps but "they banned that" — "Thank you Google for smuggling again. I CAN'T — THERE'S NO ESCAPE."

## Step 2 — Self-Hosting (No Noise, No Ads, No Product)

- "YOU GOT TO SELF-HOST, MAN. THEY'RE COMING FOR YOU."
- The key effect: "I am not the product anymore. The things I use are mine. And because they're not free, I'm not paying with my privacy or getting algo."
- "I think the main difference is that ads and news don't reach me."
- Believes in the **subconscious effect**: "Even if you are not actively processing information, just the fact that it exists in your peripheral messes with your head."
- "Self-hosting, I'm just here vibing. It's me and the tools I use. Nothing else. No noise."
- Still uses his **Steam Deck** to self-host (from de-Google video)
- "Certain services are just better to host on a VPS"
- Hosts his **website** on Hostinger

## Step 3 — Disable Shorts / Fix YouTube (Browser Extension)

- "Next thing we're going to do is we're going to disable shorts. Poof. Gone."
- "I like YouTube. I don't have to de-Google everything. But I hate how Shorts is everywhere."
- "I search — Shorts. Homepage — Shorts. I go to someone's channels — Shorts. It's just shorts."
- "If I was running a website, I would want to empower my users. Google CEO, hello. Why can't I disable if I want to? Why?"

### His Custom AI Extension

- Used his **self-trained AI** (from his previous video) to write a browser extension
- "I gave it a super simple task of just writing an extension to block Shorts for me. And bada bing, bada boom."
- Then kept adding features: "Let's keep fixing YouTube."
- What it does:
    - **Removes all Shorts**
    - **No more homepage as default** — redirects to Subscriptions feed
    - **No sidebar with recommendations** — "It's just the video"
    - Moves comments to be immediately accessible (not buried)

### The Side-by-Side Comparison

- Shows YouTube default vs. his extension while watching **Jacksepticeye** playing a game
- Default YouTube side: "Shorts. You want to watch shorts? Breaking news. You want more shorts actually, and a sponsor."
- "Oh, you wanted to go to the comments? No, you have to scroll through all these videos to get to the comments."
- "This is ridiculous. Goodness gracious. This is crazy."
- His version: "My subscription box. That's what I want to see. Not just random algorithmic choices all the freaking time."
- "YouTube as I enjoy it — connecting with people, being entertained by them, learning from them. Not this sporadic schizophrenic experience."
- "When I turn it off, I'm like oh god, is this what it used to be like?"

### Existing Alternative

- "I found out someone has already done this, so I'm going to link theirs and you can just instantly do it."
- Refers to **Unhook** ([unhook.app](http://unhook.app)) — pre-existing extension that does the same thing
- Links it in the video description so viewers can use it immediately

## Step 4 — Unfollowed Everyone on Social Media

- "I unfollowed everyone. You don't have to do this. This is definitely a me thing."

### The Myth of "Having to Be On Social Media"

- "I always had this idea that I had to be on social media. I have to — otherwise how am I going to connect with my friends?"
- His answer: "Meeting up with them... Maybe just talking to them. Maybe just ask them what they are doing instead of stalking them like a weirdo."
- "I feel like everyone has this idea that you have to be on social media. You don't have to be on social media."
- The effect of unfollowing: "I don't even bother to go because like what am I going to do? Look at memes."

### Marzia as Meme Curator

### Quitting Twitter

- "People would come to me and be like, 'Yeah, yeah, Felix is real bad.' It's like why? WHY? NO ONE'S forcing you to be on this website."

## Step 5 — DNS Blocker (Network-Level Filtering)

- "Get a DNS blocker. It's kind of the same as the YouTube thing only that it does it on a network level."
- "You can remove ads, you can remove websites — none of it even reaches your device."
- Reflects on growing up with unfiltered internet: "We grew up on the internet unfiltered and we are still unfiltered internet. I'm not crazy, man. But that's crazy."
- Can also **self-host your DNS** blocker on a VPS (mentions Hostinger makes it easy to set up)
- "You'll have a DNS blocker anywhere, which you should have."

## Step 6 — Self-Hosted RSS Feed (Intentional Doom Scroll)

## The Term

- "I think you owe it to yourself to take some time today and start building your tech fence. That's what I'm calling this — tech fencing."
- "These tech companies don't care about you. So, you're going to have to start caring about yourself."
- "I care about you. That's why I'm making this video."

## The Summary

- **"The cheat code is basically: build some friction. It's that simple."**
- "Let your brain catch up and make the choice yourself."
- "And also filter out the noise."
- **"That's your defense and that's your cure."**
- Two pillars:
    1. **Friction** — slow down the autopilot (second profile, switching delay, DNS blocking, removing algorithmic feeds)
    2. **Filtering** — remove the noise (self-hosting, extension to strip YouTube, unfollowing, RSS curation)

# 🗣️ Key Concepts & Philosophy (for TechFencing Narrative)

### Intent Is Everything

- "The key is intent. That's what matters. Intent matters."
- "If you go around your life not making your own choices, then who the heck are you?"
- "You are your choices and your actions. That's what shapes who you are. We understood this from philosophy thousands of years ago."
- It's NOT about quitting everything — "If you want to play Roblox all day, knock yourself out."
- It's about **choosing** to do things vs. **defaulting** into them

### Build Friction

- The core "cheat code" — add small delays that let your brain catch up
- 5-6 seconds of switching profiles is enough to break the autopilot
- DNS blockers prevent content from reaching you at all
- The goal: your phone serves you, not the other way around

### Filter the Noise

- Self-hosting removes ads, news, and algorithmic noise from your tools
- Curated RSS feeds replace doom scrolling with intentional content
- Browser extensions strip YouTube of its attention-grabbing design
- Unfollowing removes the algorithmic feed's power over you

### The Subconscious Effect

- "Even if you are not actively processing information, just the fact that it exists in your peripheral messes with your head."
- Even if you don't click ads or recommendations, just *seeing* them has an effect
- Self-hosting eliminates this entirely: "It's me and the tools I use. Nothing else. No noise."

### "If It's Free, You're the Product"

### The Algorithm Guy Going Against The Algorithm

- PewDiePie was made by the algorithm — became one of the biggest YouTubers because of it
- Now he's going against it: "A guy once beloved by the algo that made him a millionaire is now going against it"
- Acknowledges the irony: the success of this very video depends on the algorithm's engagement metrics
- "5,000 engineers optimizing retention. And that job has been passed down to me. I didn't ask for this."

### "You Don't Have to Be on Social Media"
